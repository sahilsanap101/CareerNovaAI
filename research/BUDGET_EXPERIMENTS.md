# Dynamic Budget Experiments

## Behavioral Impact Observations

By testing structurally identical students facing vastly asymmetrical learning budgets ($Budget_A = 40 \text{ hrs}$, $Budget_B = 400 \text{ hrs}$), we observe mathematical divergences in optimized topological sorts.

### The Low Constraint (Student A)
The system, perceiving a hard termination soon ($40 \text{hrs}$), completely abandons deep-domain structural investments (e.g. attempting to learn foundational mathematics required eventually for deep learning). The path immediately gravitates towards highly practical, high-utility operational skills possessing minimal traversal cost and low upstream blockers. The final roadmap terminates early, aggressively deferring macro-goals.

### The High Constraint (Student B)
With high bandwidth, the solver safely initiates prerequisite traversal on long, interconnected chains, confidently stacking foundational learning requirements early, knowing the global threshold supports the eventual payout downstream.

*(Algorithm outputs are explicitly determinable based purely on available temporal bandwidth).*
